How steam kettles changed shipping

Before the steam kettle, a ship's galley was a place of open fires and constant danger. The enclosed kettle changed not just the food but the architecture of the ship itself.

By 1870 nearly every long-haul vessel carried one, and insurance rates dropped accordingly. This page exists to be ordinary. It has no chat composer, no transcript, and no reason for a browser extension to touch it. If an extension restyles this page, that is a bug anyone can see.
